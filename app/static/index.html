      How it works

      1. Hear both sides with NVIDIA Parakeet speech recognition on our Nebius GPU.
      2. Check each turn: code compares numbers, doses, allergies, sides, medicines and every “no”; our fine-tuned NVIDIA checker scores whether the meaning held.
      3. Flag only what matters, in one line written by Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, quoting the exact words.
      The details Every step, and the decision rule

      1. Hear both sides

        Each turn’s audio goes to NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 on our Nebius Serverless Endpoint. In remote interpreting the interpreter is on the call channel and the room is on the device microphone, so who spoke is known.

      2. Read the critical content

        Code pulls numbers, units, how often, how long, dose limits, allergies, left and right, medicine names, warning signs and every “no” out of both languages, and compares them. “Cada seis horas” and “every 6 hours” match; “cada ocho horas” doesn’t.

      3. Weigh the meaning

        Our checker, NVIDIA llama-nemotron-rerank-1b-v2 fine-tuned on a Nebius Serverless Job, scores whether the interpretation still means the same thing. It’s there for what words alone miss, like “not this week” becoming “this week you can”; the results show how often it does.

      4. Decide

        A fixed rule turns both into one of three outcomes: a re-confirm card, a note for after the visit, or nothing. A changed allergy, “no”, medicine, side or dose limit is always a card. The rule’s two thresholds are picked on held-out practice visits, never on the test sets.

      5. Say it plainly

        For cards only, Nemotron 3.5 Lightning writes one line for the interpreter and clinician. Code checks that the words it quotes were really said.

      6. Re-read after the visit

        Nemotron 3 Ultra reads the whole conversation once and lists anything the turn checks missed, each tied to a quote that code verifies.

      What this is not Not an interpreter, no medical advice, nothing stored
      • Not an interpreter, and no substitute for a qualified one. The human interpreter does every clarification.
      • It never speaks to the patient and never gives medical advice.
      • No scores or reports on interpreters. Nothing is recorded or stored.
      • Spanish and English only. Speakers of Indigenous languages such as Mam or K’iche’ aren’t covered.
      • Built and tested on synthetic and scripted visits. Nebius Serverless isn’t HIPAA-eligible by default, so real patient audio needs a different deployment.
      • A speech-recognition slip can look like an interpreting error, so cards say “re-confirm”, never “wrong”.
